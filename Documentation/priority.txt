1. Strategic Framework & Business Environment

Institutional Context (PK Fokam Research Center & MFIs)
The MICROFI project is developed under the aegis of the PK Fokam Research Center in
partnership with Microfinance Institutions (MFIs) across the CEMAC zone. The strategic
objective is to design a standardized, modular, and agnostic technology infrastructure to
address the challenges of rural and semi-urban financial inclusion.
The CEMAC zone is characterized by a strong duality in the microfinance sector:
CATEGORY 1 — Cooperatives & Mutual Networks

MC² / MUFFA networks, village cooperatives, and GICs, historically operating on decentralized foundations with a heavy reliance on paper and infrastructure constraints (intermittent
connectivity, rural remoteness).
CATEGORY 2 — Corporations & Centralized MFIs

Regulated commercial MFIs equipped with centralized Information Systems (Sopra Amplitude, Kovara, Asengo) but suffering from rigidity in expanding their proximity collection
channels.

Stakeholder Mapping (Stakeholders Analysis & RACI Matrix)
The success of the project depends on rigorous coordination among the various internal and
external actors of the financial ecosystem.
RACI legend: R = Responsible (executes the task), A = Accountable (validates / accountable), C = Consulted (provides
expertise), I = Informed (kept up to date).

Business Risk Analysis & Regulatory Constraints (COBAC / CEMAC)
Proximity collection activity in the CEMAC zone is governed by strict requirements set out by
COBAC and BEAC regarding anti-money laundering, fund security, and customer protection.
CASH TRACEABILITY

Obligation to link every physical collection to an identified client and to ensure rigorous
traceability of banknote denominations (BEAC).
CASH-HANDLING EXPOSURE CEILINGS

High exposure of field agents to physical assault and cash theft.
COBAC COMPLIANCE

Requirement for daily accounting reconciliation and a formal ban on holding unrecorded
funds at the end of the day.

Diagnosis of Dysfunctions & Bottlenecks
An audit of current practices reveals five major systemic failures:
• Opacity of collection & “cavalerie” risk — In the absence of immediate digital traceability, some agents use funds collected from Client B to cover a late payment or unauthorized
disbursement owed by Client A (a form of check-kiting / rolling shortfall).
• Slow Evening reconciliation / cash counting — Manual counting of heterogeneous masses
of banknotes by cashiers creates enormous queues and human summation errors.
• Counterfeit notes and disputes — When a counterfeit note is detected in the evening at the
branch, it is impossible to attribute it to a specific client, creating financial disputes and tension
directed at the agent.
• Lack of GPS visibility and escrow overruns — Agents often collect beyond their actual financial guarantee without automated control, exposing the MFI to outright losses in the event of
misappropriation or theft.
• Information asymmetry — Client account balances on the Core Banking System (CBS) remain
stale throughout the entire day of field operations.

Competitive Technology Landscape
To strategically position MICROFI within the microfinance landscape of Cameroon and the
CEMAC zone, this section presents a comparative analysis of the technologies currently
used in the field, followed by MICROFI’s unique positioning.
The proximity-collection market today relies on three major technology families.
A — Dedicated Payment Terminals (POS / Portable POS)

Standalone physical devices distributed to agents (e.g., traditional pre-smartphone solutions).
Advantages: Very robust physically; natively integrate a thermal printer to deliver an immediate paper receipt (a major trust factor for older or illiterate merchants).
Disadvantages: Very high acquisition and hardware-maintenance cost for the MFI; limited
functionality; absolute dependence on a data SIM card; no advanced GPS tracking.

B — Local Fintech Smartphone Apps (e.g., Smobilpay, Blucash)

Android mobile applications connected to Bluetooth mini-printers.
Advantages: Flexible and field-adapted (offline mode often available); reduced hardware

The client saves on their own by dialing a code (e.g., *126# or *150#) from their own

Advantages: Zero risk of physical cash theft; no field-agent logistics required; 24/7 availability.
Disadvantages: High transaction fees charged by operators (MTN/Orange) that discourage
small daily savings; dehumanization of the relationship (market merchants prefer the daily
visit of a trusted human collector).

system.

BR-02

Denomination Breakdown Rules — Entry of the denomination breakdown (10,000, 5,000,
2,000, 1,000, 500 XAF, and coins) is mandatory for any deposit above a defined threshold.
The sum of the breakdown must strictly match the total amount entered.
BR-04

Digital Booklet Tokenization & Agent-Sponsored Activation — Existing MFI members
shall activate their mobile profile using a temporary Activation ID issued by the Core Banking
System. Activation (and annual renewal) requires sponsorship by a registered Field Agent
and payment of a configurable fee that is automatically split between the agent (commission)
and the MFI. The resulting digital access token is valid for 365 days. Upon expiry the mobile
profile enters a read-only state (balances remain visible; transactional operations are locked)
until renewed. The system shall never create new customer records inside the Core Banking
System.
BR-05

Mandatory GPS for Collection — No collection transaction shall be initiated or validated if
the agent terminal does not have an active GPS/location service providing a current fix. If
GPS is disabled, denied by the operating system, or unable to produce a valid position, the
mobile application shall block the collection flow and display a clear message requiring the
agent to enable location services. Network-based approximate location alone is not sufficient
to authorize a new collection.

FR-01

Single Sign-On Authentication — The system shall authenticate the field agent via a unique
identifier tied to the hardware address (IMEI) of their smartphone.
FR-02

Admin Enrollment — The MFI administrator shall be able to create, modify, suspend, or
delete an agent’s profile from the central console.

MODULE 2 — Escrow & Financial Lock Management

as the cumulative cash collected reaches the active escrow amount.
FR-05

Exceptional Adjustment — The administrator shall be able to temporarily modify an agent’s
collection ceiling remotely during the course of the day.

MODULE 2 — Escrow & Financial Lock Management

FR-03 — Multi-Bank Top-Up
User Story (US-03): As a Collection Agent (or Administrator), I want to top up my digital escrow
wallet via Mobile Money or interbank transfer so that I can increase my authorized collection
ceiling without returning to the branch.
UC-03

Name: Escrow Wallet Top-Up (Multi-Channel)
Actors: Collection Agent, Administrator, Payment Gateway (GIMAC / Mobile Money)
Objectives: Increase the agent’s available escrow balance securely and in near real-time.
Preconditions: Agent profile is active; at least one payment channel is configured for the
MFI.
Postconditions: Escrow balance is increased; lockout threshold is updated; transaction is
logged and visible on Back-Office.
Nominal Scenario:
1. Agent (or Admin) initiates top-up from the Wallet screen.
2. Agent selects channel (Orange Money, MTN MoMo, or GIMAC transfer) and enters
amount.
3. System generates a payment reference and redirects or displays USSD/instructions.
4. Payment provider confirms the transfer.
5. System credits the escrow wallet and raises the collection ceiling.
Alternative Scenarios:
• A1 — Payment failure: System leaves balance unchanged and notifies the agent; retry is
possible.
• A2 — Offline initiation: Request is queued; credit is applied only after successful online
confirmation.
• A3 — Manual cashier top-up (MVP fallback): Cashier records a physical deposit; Administrator validates and credits the wallet.
Business Rules: BR-Escrow-01: Top-up amounts must be positive and within configured
limits. BR-Escrow-02: Full multi-bank automation is backlog; MVP supports single-channel
+ manual recording.

ANALYST OVERRIDE NOTE

FR-07 (Offline Collection Mode) scores below the 3.9 MVP threshold once Development
Speed is factored in, because building reliable local encrypted storage and background sync
takes real engineering time. However, offline capability is the architectural foundation the
entire platform is built on — collection genuinely happens in low-connectivity markets, and
FR-06 (all client identification channels), FR-08, and FR-12 all assume it. A purely mechanical score cannot be allowed to cut a load-bearing dependency; FR-07 is therefore forceincluded in MVP regardless of its numeric ranking. This is the one deliberate exception to
the scoring model in this release.

5. Conclusion
The analysis conducted in this document has highlighted the major limitations of the current
proximity collection process used by microfinance institutions: strong dependence on manual operations, data entry errors, difficulties in end-of-day reconciliation, lack of transaction traceability,
and limited operational visibility over field agents’ activities.
In response to these challenges, the proposed MICROFI solution provides an approach adapted
to the operational realities of microfinance institutions in the CEMAC region. By relying on a
digital-first and offline-first approach, the platform ensures business continuity even in environments characterized by limited connectivity. The transformation from the AS-IS process to the
TO-BE process therefore introduces a higher level of control, security, and efficiency.
The integration of features such as secure agent enrollment, guarantee and collateral management, transaction geolocation, real-time collection monitoring, automated reconciliation, and
export capabilities toward existing banking systems addresses the main business requirements
identified. The solution also improves governance through a clear definition of responsibilities
among stakeholders according to a RACI approach.
The decision to adopt an incremental development strategy through an MVP makes it possible
to control project risks while delivering operational value quickly to partner institutions. This first
version provides an evolutionary foundation capable of supporting future advanced features such
as intelligent fraud detection using machine learning, complete integration with Mobile Money
networks, and predictive analysis of agent performance.
Therefore, MICROFI is not merely a collection digitization tool, but a true operational transformation platform enabling microfinance institutions to improve efficiency, strengthen customer
trust, and achieve better risk management.

5. Glossary
Alphabetical list of abbreviations and key terms used in the Business Analysis and in the System
Architecture and Software Design Document.
ACID Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability. Set of properties that guarantee reliable database
transactions (used for financial ledger modules via Spring Data JPA).
AES Advanced Encryption Standard. Symmetric encryption algorithm; MICROFI uses AES-256
for offline local storage on agent terminals (NFR-01).
AML Anti-Money Laundering. Legal and operational measures to prevent financial institutions
from being used for money laundering.
AMQP Advanced Message Queuing Protocol. Messaging protocol used by RabbitMQ to decouple
the Core Backend from the CBS Middleware.
API Application Programming Interface. Rules and protocols allowing software systems to communicate and exchange data.
AS-IS Representation of the current state of a business process before transformation (existing
practices, workflows and limitations).
BEAC Bank of Central African States. Central bank for monetary policy and financial regulation
in the CEMAC region.
BOLA Broken Object-Level Authorization. Access-control flaw where a caller can access or modify
objects belonging to another user or branch; addressed by server-side branch scoping.
BR Business Rule. Constraint or policy that the system must enforce (e.g. BR-01 OFJ delta, BR-03
escrow lock, BR-04 digital booklet token, BR-05 mandatory GPS).
Branch Cashier Staff member responsible for physical cash at the branch, counting, reconciliation and validation of daily collections.
CBS Core Banking System. Central system managing customer accounts, ledgers and financial
operations (e.g. Amplitude, FinanSoft).
CEMAC Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa. Regional community with shared
monetary and financial regulations.
CFT Counter-Terrorism Financing. Measures to prevent financing of terrorism through the financial system.
COBAC Central African Banking Commission. Supervisory authority for banking regulation and
prudential control in CEMAC.
CRUD Create, Read, Update, Delete. The four basic operations on data entities.
DDL Data Definition Language. SQL statements that define schema objects (tables, indexes, constraints).
Digital Cash Desk Digital process that replaces manual cash reconciliation by comparing recorded
collections with physical cash (OFJ).
